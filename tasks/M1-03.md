## Outcome and boundaries

Decode one representative segment of the track the validated race scenario runs on (DRAGSTER in the CRAWLER tour) and the rider frames the scenario draws for MIKE, from the ROM through the loading path the original executes, and show that the decoded data is what the original uses at runtime. Record where the data comes from and how it is transformed. Nothing is extracted into tracked files.

**Part A — provenance: how the data reaches the PPU and work RAM.** Using `access capture` on the race scenario with the DMA registers and the block-move site watched: list every DMA transfer set up during the race load (frames 1200–1400) and during riding — channel, direction, B-bus register (`$2118`/`$2119` VRAM, `$2122` CGRAM, `$2104` OAM, or work RAM), A-bus source (bank, address), size, and the pc that started it (`$420B` write) — and every block move through `$00:0199` (source, destination, length, the operand banks as read from the end-of-frame work RAM, labelled as in D-0002). From these, name the ROM regions (file offsets under the LoROM rule) that supply the DRAGSTER track tiles, tile map, palette and MIKE's sprite tiles, and whether each is copied raw or passes through a decompressor in work RAM first (a transfer whose source is work RAM that was itself filled by a routine reading ROM through indexed loads points at a decompressor; name the routine's entry point from the coverage map and the ROM range it reads). Record the reads of ROM by the physics code during riding (from the access record: reads with sources in banks above $83 or in the referenced ROM addresses of the race map) as the candidate collision or track-geometry data, without decoding them if the budget does not allow.

**Part B — decode and runtime comparison.** Decode one segment: the tiles and tile-map region visible in the frame image at a chosen riding frame (for example frame 1600, before the rider has moved far, and one later frame), and the rider tiles the OAM upload contains, from the ROM offsets of part A, through the identified transformation (raw copy, or a reimplementation of the decompressor with its arithmetic preserved and checked byte-for-byte against the work RAM it produced in the original run). Compare against the original's runtime rendering: render the decoded tiles with the decoded palette at the positions the tile map gives for the visible region and compare pixel-for-pixel against the frame image (`--frame-image`) over a stated rectangle, stating the PPU features the renderer omits (priority, scrolling registers, windowing, sprite priority) and the resulting mismatch count; a byte-for-byte comparison of the decoded data with the work RAM staging buffer (from the work RAM series) where one exists is the stronger check and is preferred where available. If a collision or geometry table is identified in part A, compare one value the physics reads (from the access record's load values) with the decoded table entry at that index.

Deliverables: `docs/content/dragster-segment.md` (identifiers, ROM offsets and sizes, format description, the transformation, the comparison results and their limits; the stable logical identifiers for the track, segment, palette and rider frames independent of ROM offsets, per the project plan); `tests/manifests/content/` (a manifest of the decoded items with ROM digest, offsets, sizes and the expected digest of the decoded bytes, so that a fresh host regenerates and verifies them without any tracked content); the `content` commands (`content provenance`, `content decode`, `content compare` or as adjusted) with ROM-free tests on synthetic data for the decoder and the tile renderer; R-0008; this record's handoff.

Not in scope: other tracks, tours or riders; a general extractor; audio; a renderer beyond what the comparison needs; any native gameplay; a core patch change (D-0002 fallback: if VRAM contents are needed beyond what the frame image and the DMA parameters show, record the need and stop that branch); assigning meaning to work RAM fields (M1-02).

Tracked-content rule: no ROM bytes, decoded tiles, palettes, tile maps or images are tracked; tracked files carry offsets, sizes, formats, digests, identifiers and counts. Decoded content and comparison images stay in ignored `artifacts/`.
